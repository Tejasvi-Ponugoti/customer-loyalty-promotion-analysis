# Portfolio Presentation

The project presentation is maintained as a separate portfolio artefact.

## Executive summary

**Question:** Which customer groups react most to grocery promotions, and are those promotions commercially worthwhile?

### Scale
- 2,576,815 cleaned transaction lines
- 2,500 households
- 275,539 shopping baskets
- 13,249 products included in the promotion comparison

### Customer insight
Four RFM/K-means groups were created:
- Low-Value — 400 households; 2.03% of spend
- Occasional — 521 households; 8.85% of spend
- Regular — 695 households; 20.68% of spend
- Loyal High-Value — 884 households; 68.44% of spend

### Promotion finding
Promoted weeks were associated with **58.34% higher unit sales overall** than non-promoted weeks under this methodology. Low-Value customers showed the highest measured uplift (63.63%), while Loyal High-Value customers accounted for most spending and showed 57.86% measured uplift.

### Commercial finding
At an assumed 25% gross margin:
- estimated incremental sales: ~£1.021m
- observed store-discount cost: ~£958.6k
- estimated net result: ~-£703.4k

The recommendation is therefore **not** “promotions do not work.” The analysis suggests promotions are associated with higher unit demand, but broad discounting does not cover its estimated cost under the scenario assumptions. A better next step is targeted promotion testing using matched controls or a randomised design.

## Caveat
These are observational comparisons. They do not establish causal promotion impact.
